Ancient Aromatic Resin

What is Olibanum?

Olibanum, which is globally recognized as Frankincense and locally known as luban, is a prized aromatic resin harvested from the scraggy but remarkably hardy Boswellia Papyrifera tree.

The resin is collected through a careful process known as striping, where the bark is strategically slashed to encourage the oleo-gum to bleed out and harden on the tree. These formations of hardened resin are called tears, which typically present as irregular nodules ranging in color from a pale greenish hue to rich yellow. These tears form a solid, crystalline mass that may yield a slightly plastic quality when pressed.

Linguistic Sourcing Heritage

Etan & Gum Olibanum

Resin nomenclature carries historical weight. Understanding the connection between international trade labels and native names highlights the resin's deep-rooted heritage:

English: Gum Olibanum

Derived from the Arabic term al-lubán ("the milk"), referring to the milky sap that exudes from the bark. This is the standard trade name utilized in global shipping documents, quality certifications, and cosmetic formulations.

Amharic: Etan (ዕጣን)

The local Ethiopian name, representing the native culture and history surrounding the Boswellia tree. It is burnt daily in traditional coffee ceremonies and spiritual programs throughout Ethiopia, signifying hospitality, peace, and cleansing.
